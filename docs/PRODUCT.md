# Product overview

PACKETLOSS is a work-in-progress maze arcade game for desktop and mobile browsers. The core experience is navigating a maze, recovering data bits, avoiding enemies, and turning the chase around with power cores. It uses animated 3D meshes and a following camera. Classic maps are authored in Tiled; Endless generates new maze sections during play.

## Gameplay

### Movement and mazes

The Packet moves along the maze corridors. Perpendicular direction inputs queue the next turn, which takes effect at the next center where the path allows it; reversing direction takes effect immediately within the current corridor. Walls and maze boundaries constrain movement. In Classic, tutorial, and Endless wordmark sections, linked portals let the Packet and enemies other than Virus travel between parts of the maze; the Packet briefly blinks and is protected from harmful enemy collisions after teleporting.

### Data and scoring

- Data bits are worth 10 points and disappear when collected.
- Power cores are worth 50 points and temporarily make active enemies scared.
- The Packet collects either pickup as soon as its body touches it, even before reaching the tile center.
- Eating scared enemies awards increasing bonuses: 200, 400, 800, then 1,600 points.
- A new power core refreshes the scared period and resets the enemy bonus chain.
- Each normal level starts with all five score-multiplier icons on preset packet-reachable tiles: two pairs mirrored across the map's vertical centerline and one on the centerline, all away from the Packet spawn. Each mirrored pair is within one boost window's travel of its partner. Icons stay until collected or the level ends and do not count toward clearing the maze.
- Multiplier icons use the same six-second rotation as power cores. Data bits underneath available icons are hidden visually; their collection, points, and maze-completion rules stay the same.
- The five icons are bug ×1.5, key ×2, cloud ×2.5, wifi ×3, and chip ×4. Collecting one grants no immediate points, but multiplies all data-bit, power-core, and scared-enemy awards for 15 seconds of simulation time. Collecting another during an active boost multiplies the current icon factor by that pickup's factor and resets the timer to 15 seconds (bug followed by key gives ×3). After a boost expires, the next pickup starts at its own factor. Death and level clear end an active boost; uncollected icons remain through death. Pause freezes the boost timer.

The first level uses these base awards and normal simulation speed. Continuing after a clear compounds both the entire gameplay simulation and every score award by 25% per level. Movement consumes the full scaled travel distance while still resolving each crossed center, so fractional multipliers do not round actor speed down. Awards are calculated as base value × level factor × active icon factor, then rounded once to the nearest integer. The HUD shows the number of available icons or the active combined boost with whole seconds remaining. Active play time remains wall-clock time rather than scaled simulation time.

Generated data bits fill every eligible reachable tile, including straight corridors and wider floor areas. Large power cores are scarce: 12 in the default maze and four in the demo. They favor outer corner bends, then well-separated interior bends, away from the player spawn, portal mouths, and jail entrances. Explicitly map-authored collectibles keep their authored positions.

### Enemies and lives

The default roster starts with one Firewall, Virus, Ping, Spam, Lag, Quarantine, and Trojan, released from the pen at the existing staggered intervals. Each body is lethal on contact. While scared, enemies move at half their own normal speed and can be eaten. Eaten enemies spend 420 ms collapsing into tiny, harmless, wide-eyed bugs with scurrying feet, then move back to the pen at twice their own normal speed. They follow the shortest physical corridor route, including access through the prison bars, without teleporting through portals. Original enemies regain their bodies only after reaching the jail center and enter the usual release cycle; Spam copies disappear on arrival. Returning bugs cannot be eaten again and do not use enemy abilities. A flashing warning signals when the scared period is ending.

| Enemy | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Firewall | Moves 15% faster than the Packet. While in the pen, chooses a random reachable patrol destination. After release it travels there and repeats a right-hand loop of at least 16 tile steps, independent of the player. It chooses again for every release and rejoins its current loop after scared mode. It does not physically block other enemies. |
| Virus | Pursues the player at 85% of Packet speed using the shortest physical corridor route, including reversals but never portals. |
| Ping | Every three seconds, detects the player through walls within eight tiles and pursues that last detected position. A pulse and brief target marker show successful detection. Other enemies receive no alert. |
| Spam | Attempts to split every four seconds into an unoccupied adjacent tile. Copies can reproduce, up to four Spam total. Blocked attempts retry after one second. Copies are 80% size and award the normal scared-enemy chain score when eaten. |
| Lag | Roams at half normal enemy speed and drops a zone on newly traversed tiles at most once per second, with at most two active zones. New drops wait until a zone expires. Zones last twenty seconds and halve player speed without stacking. The body is lethal; zones only slow. |
| Quarantine | Roams and attempts to extend nearby maze walls across two open passages every five seconds, up to four active extensions. Connected walls last seven seconds and may temporarily trap the Packet or enemies; other tile exits remain open. They never appear through actors, on portal mouths, or around jail exits. Purple outlines and a brief construction beam identify their source. |
| Trojan | Roams, then every six seconds chooses a reachable empty tile the Packet has traversed, walks there at its normal speed, and transforms into a data bit in place. Its tile and all eight neighboring tiles must contain no real collectible, and the ambush starts more than three tiles from the Packet. It rechecks the site on arrival and retries after one second if no suitable site remains. Other enemies cannot trigger the fake bit. It remains still until the Packet comes within two tiles or ten seconds pass, then reveals its horse form with a 600 ms stationary, harmless escape window before normal contact danger resumes. Fake bits award no score and do not count toward maze completion. |

Power cores disable detection, splitting, new Lag zones, Quarantine walls, and Trojan disguises. They immediately clear existing Lag zones and Quarantine walls and reveal disguised Trojans as edible horses. Ability cooldowns restart after release and scared mode. There are at most ten enemies: the seven originals and three reusable Spam copies. Abilities and lingering effects continue during player death and freeze while paused. Respawning removes temporary walls and disguises; a new level also clears the visited-route history used by Trojan. Later-level speed applies to movement, releases, abilities, power windows, death/recovery, effects, and gameplay animation, while movement still stops exactly at tile centers to preserve queued turns and check every crossed corridor for pickups.

The player starts with three lives. Contact with a dangerous enemy costs one life and holds the Packet at the contact position for a 900 ms level-one digital-glitch death animation. Its body stays recognizable as it stutters through horizontally displaced cyan and magenta scan slices and echoes, scrambled binary digits, and brief signal dropouts, then cuts out. During this phase the Packet cannot move, collect data, or suffer another hit; enemies and gameplay timers continue. With lives remaining, the Packet then returns to its spawn and begins the usual blinking recovery period that prevents another immediate hit. Every original enemy is restored to its initial jail placement, Spam copies retire, transient enemy effects are cleared, and the normal staggered release cycle restarts. At zero lives, the death animation finishes without a respawn or enemy reset, the Packet stays hidden, and the run ends with **PACKET LOST**. Pausing freezes the death timer and animation. Score and lives appear in the HUD.

Collecting the last data pickup pauses the run at a **MAZE CLEARED** checkpoint, including that pickup's scaled points. The checkpoint states **GAME IS NOW SPED UP AND SCORING IS INCREASED** without presenting final-run statistics. Dangerous contact is resolved before collection and blocks pickup on that tick. A map that starts without collectibles does not automatically award a clear. The cleared maze remains empty behind the checkpoint until the player selects **Continue**. Continuing refills the exact original collectible layout, resets the Packet and enemies, preserves score and remaining lives, and starts the next level at 1.25 times the prior level's simulation speed and scoring. The multiplier compounds without a level cap. Leaving from a clear checkpoint requires confirmation and abandons the unsaved run.

Only losing all lives completes and records a new multi-level Classic run. The loss result freezes the simulation and shows final score, local best, cumulative data recovered, levels cleared, and active play time. Play time includes active death and recovery but excludes loading, pauses, and clear checkpoints. Historical clear records remain readable.

### Endless mode

The title menu’s **Start game** opens a compact mode menu whose header is the animated PACKETLOSS wordmark, with no additional Start game title, above **Endless** and **Classic**. Endless is highlighted as the default each time the menu opens, while keyboard focus remains on the panel until the player navigates; activating either option immediately starts that mode. Back or Escape returns to the title with focus on its panel rather than an action. Endless uses a 25-tile-wide maze that continues upward and downward between side rails like the Classic perimeter. Its permanent corridors have no dead ends or one-entrance pockets: every area has at least two physical ways out. Quarantine walls may still create temporary traps. No physical vertical route runs through five consecutive tiles, including across section joins. Horizontal walls have a passage break before spanning more than ten corridor columns. Parallel lanes, staggered junctions, nested turns, and long loops vary from section to section. The Packet starts at the center of a full-width portal corridor directly beneath a centered PACKETLOSS wordmark; other sections may contain full-width crossings or long straight stretches. Subsequent sections have a 50% chance of a complete, horizontally centered wordmark, subject to at least 24 rows between wordmarks. The row directly below each sign is an uninterrupted left-to-right corridor with working portals at its side openings, framed by equally long straight wall spans above and below; data bits and bonuses can still appear along it. The portals use the same blink and protection behavior as Classic, while Virus and returning bugs stay on physical routes. Five 24-row sections stay resident around the Packet. Geometry and uncollected pickups in retained sections persist when reversing direction; a discarded distant section gets a fresh layout and pickups if revisited. There is no enemy pen, maze-clear checkpoint, or level refill.

Data bits fill eligible corridors. Roughly one power core appears per 180 eligible tiles, and each section has one random multiplier icon using the existing awards, boost duration, and stacking behavior. Discarding an uncollected section does not add to recovered data. Score, recovered-data count, and active play time accumulate until the final loss.

The whole Endless simulation starts at normal 1× speed and increases with every score point using `sqrt(1 + score / 10,000)`, without milestones or a cap. This reaches about 1.41× at 10,000 points and 1.73× at 20,000; each additional point contributes less speed as the run progresses. Score awards do not rise with speed. After five active simulation seconds, enemy waves attempt to enter at random 5–9 simulation-second intervals. Entering each previously unexplored 24-row section in either direction lowers both interval bounds by 0.5 seconds, down to 2–4 seconds. Backtracking and regenerated sections do not add progress. Each wave has one to three available original enemies in a single entry, a staggered group, or a split group. Four in five waves prefer a reachable offscreen corridor ahead of the Packet’s current direction; the rest vary their approach. If a forward entrance is unavailable, the wave uses another safe entrance, and delayed enemies reconsider the Packet’s direction before appearing. Enemies retire well after leaving view. The seven original archetypes and three reusable Spam-copy slots keep the population at ten or fewer. Newly arriving enemies inherit the remaining power-core effect. Eaten enemies finish the existing intake animation, then flee through physical corridors as harmless bugs until offscreen.

The Packet starts with three lives. After a nonfinal 900 ms death animation, it continues from the exact collision position and direction, nearby enemies and temporary hazards clear, encounters wait another five seconds, and the existing 1,200 ms flashing recovery protects it. The final death ends the run. Endless results show score, its separate local best, recovered-data count, and active play time. Restart, retry, and replay keep Endless selected.

## Battle Royale multiplayer

Battle Royale is a private, authenticated room for two to four players. Every match uses a freshly seeded 49 × 49 maze: the left-side passages are randomized and mirrored on the right, while the PACKETLOSS lettering stays at `(22…26,24)`. The horizontal portal lane remains on row 25, the upper and lower vertical lanes remain on column 24, and fixed side passages route around the lettering. Short matching straight wall spans frame the passages above the lettering and below its horizontal corridor, with center gaps for the vertical lanes; the surrounding maze stays procedural. There is no enemy jail. Four reviewed spawn candidates remain at `(11,21)`, `(37,21)`, `(11,30)`, and `(37,30)`. Two-player matches use the upper pair; larger matches add the lower pair. The server randomizes the first assignment and rotates assignments on rematches.

The arena warns continuously and closes one tile inward on every side after each ten seconds of play. Its paired horizontal and vertical portal mouths move to the new boundary. Every generated layout remains physically connected at all 21 sizes. Each surviving corridor keeps at least two exits; an active portal mouth can count its working portal as one. Seeded short vertical wall spans flank the full center lane above and below PACKETLOSS, with openings into the surrounding maze. The maze reaches 9 × 9 after 200 seconds and the match has a 240-second limit. The current outer wall uses the same solid wall profile as Classic, with normal pink outlines until a wall-closure countdown is active. During each ten-second countdown, its rails, corners, and portal tips, plus every interior wall segment that the next closure removes and its complete end caps, turn purple and pulse progressively faster, from 0.5 to 2.5 cycles per second, using Quarantine’s outline effect. Surviving interior wall segments stay steady. Each closure immediately removes the retired walls and floors and starts the next perimeter’s countdown pulse slowly again. The final perimeter and walls outside active play return to steady pink. Reduced motion keeps the countdown outline steadily purple. The camera follows the player smoothly through maze edges and contractions, allowing empty space outside the retained maze to remain visible.

Every participant, including local bots, receives a random unused Packet variant when joining: sky blue, rose, lime, or violet, with no additional accessories. All use the solo Packet's beveled body, animated eyes, binary details, movement trail, hunter form, and death effect. Each player's assigned color stays consistent across the lobby, play, reconnects, and rematches, returning after the temporary gold hunting effect. Packets have no ground ring. Variants are cosmetic and have identical movement and collision rules. Solo modes keep their original cyan Packet.

Online Packets display their player name just above the character's center and their server-confirmed score just below it. Labels face the camera and remain readable while the body blinks during protection. Cosmetic effects grow through ten score steps: Quiet (0), Trace (250), Spark (500), Signal (1,000), Stream (2,000), Charge (3,500), Surge (5,000), Radiant (6,500), Critical (8,000), and Overload (10,000+). Quiet and Trace have no decorative binary digits; subsequent steps progressively add 0s and 1s, particles, stronger trails, and brighter glow. These thresholds are shared presentation configuration, not gameplay bonuses. A fresh match resets the appearance with the score.

Battle Royale has no enemy actors, player-versus-player damage, or contact damage. Data bits and twelve mirrored power cores are shared and can be awarded only once; a seeded participant order resolves otherwise simultaneous claims. Bits award 10 points. Collecting a core awards 50 points and gives only its collector a six-second gold Packet effect. Multiplier icons and level scaling are disabled. Pickups in a retired layer disappear, and an emptied layout refills only the currently active corridors.

A Packet outside the new playable boundary at a closure is eliminated after the existing death effect. All affected players are evaluated together. One survivor finishes the match immediately; if a closure eliminates everyone, the result states **No survivors** and players eliminated together share placement. At the time limit, survivors rank first by score, followed by eliminated players in survival order. Score ties use competition ranks such as `1, 1, 3`, and every starter remains in the final standings.

Disconnecting freezes a surviving Packet in place while retaining its vulnerability to the next closure and reserving the slot for 30 seconds. A valid reconnect resumes the exact position; eliminated players reconnect as spectators. Intentional departure or reservation expiry eliminates the participant. A spectator automatically follows the remaining player with the lowest slot number.

The game server owns the generated map, movement, pickups, shrink stages, eliminations, scores, and deadline. Focus changes and menus suppress local input without pausing the match. The browser predicts only its own movement, resets presentation correction when topology changes, and treats the server snapshot as authoritative. Rooms admit new players only before the match starts, transfer controls when the creator leaves, generate a fresh map for rematches, and preserve completed multiplayer results separately from single-player records. An account- and server-generation-bound tab-session room code keeps the reserved reconnect action available after a page refresh; every reconnect still requires a new authenticated single-use ticket.

**Tutorial** appears immediately below **About** in the main menu and launches guided practice directly. About contains instructions and enemy information without a tutorial launch button. Practice uses the existing demo maze regardless of the configured game map, and never saves scores or records.

Each lesson opens paused with a short explanation and **Try it**. During play, the lesson number, one objective, and cyan floor markers identify what to do. The movement lesson marks every remaining data bit; other lessons retain their single current target. Lessons advance on actual gameplay milestones:

| --- | --- |
| Movement and data | Start moving automatically and recover the marked data bits at `(1,1)`, `(1,11)`, `(6,5)`, and `(11,3)`. |
| Meet Firewall | Collect the data bit at `(1,2)` while Firewall travels to and follows its seeded long patrol. |
| Meet Virus | Reach the data bit at `(11,11)` while Virus follows its chase route. |
| Meet Ping | Start moving automatically, observe a successful scan, then recover the data bit at `(10,11)`. |

| Meet Trojan | Clear a row, inspect its fake data bit, approach to reveal the horse, then escape beyond its reach during the brief harmless window. |

Each lesson and retry creates fresh practice with the lesson's enemies and collectibles, normal movement speeds, and real ability timings. Every lesson starts the Packet moving in an intended open direction as soon as practice resumes; it stops only when maze collision blocks that direction until the player steers. The practice camera fits the small demo maze to the viewport so the player can observe the staged enemies. Cyan target boxes render over maze walls so their locations remain visible. Explanations within a lesson freeze its current scene. Success advances immediately to the next lesson without an outro; dangerous contact or an expired required effect freezes play and offers unlimited retries. Manual pause and focus handling remain available, but shortcuts or focus return cannot dismiss a checkpoint. Exiting or cancelling loading returns to the main menu with Tutorial focused. Completion of the final lesson offers **Start game**, **Replay tutorial**, and **Back to main menu**.

## Presentation

### Canonical HTML visual contract

Every new or changed HTML interface must match the 3D scene and reuse the shared CSS tokens and Tailwind 4 utilities and styles in `src/game/ui/gameUi.css`. Menu interfaces also reuse the React components and header/body/actions/footer slots in `src/game/ui/MenuPanel.tsx`. Apply this to menus, the HUD, loading/error states, and future changes to development tools.

- Use near-black surfaces (`#02040b`, `#050912`), muted pink (`#b579a1`), cyan (`#419da9`), and gold (`#b9a36b`). Reserve brighter cyan for primary actions and keyboard focus; keep body text readable against the dark surfaces.
- Use Orbitron headings with readable supporting text, square corners, thin outlines, and small corner-bracket details. Keep decoration restrained and controls clearly labelled.
- Use the same panel frame, spacing, and corner treatment throughout: standard panels have a maximum width of 640 pixels; the mode menu uses a compact 480-pixel maximum with reduced padding and spacing; About and Profile & records use the 1,040-pixel wide variant. Shared text headers are left-aligned; the title and mode wordmarks remain centered. Submenu Back controls are 48-pixel icon buttons aligned in the same row. The main and pause menus share one vertical stack of full-width buttons and left-aligned supporting text. About sections and profile record lists share the responsive column layout, stacking below 800 pixels.
- Keep the frozen scene visible behind pause and result panels under a dark scrim. Do not desaturate or filter the entire game mount, which would also alter HTML colors.
- Use brief, restrained entrances: a signal-break effect for loss and a gold accent sweep for success. Honor the menu motion preference and system reduced-motion setting.
- Menus stay gently animated with drifting maze lines, softly shifting panel shadows, pulsing corner outlines, and scattered 0/1 digits that drift, fade, and switch at staggered intervals like the Packet's glyphs. Keep the ambient background mounted across menu navigation so changing screens does not restart its animation. Leave decoration visible at the edges and gutters around wide panels, behind opaque content, hidden from assistive technology, and unable to intercept input. Reduced motion leaves it static. These CSS effects use no gameplay timers and disappear during play; the paused 3D scene remains frozen.
- Panels stay in place and fade in without vertical movement. Buttons lift 2 pixels on hover or keyboard focus and depress 1 pixel when pressed; the primary action's border brightens over 10 seconds and dims over the next 10 seconds while idle. Keep these responses subtle, with no repeated button movement while idle, and disable their motion with the reduced-motion setting.
- Every shared menu frame includes simultaneous faint sweeps along its top and bottom edges in opposite directions, plus a small pulse of square pixels in its bottom corner. Both sweeps share one animation so their movement and fading stay synchronized. Use the panel's shared accent for its corner brackets and signal decoration: cyan normally, pink for loss, and gold for a clear. Keep these effects outside the content, hidden from assistive technology, and static under reduced motion; reuse `MenuPanel` and CSS keyframes instead of creating timers for individual screens.
- Provide visible keyboard focus, focus containment/restoration for menus, touch targets of at least 44 pixels, mobile safe-area spacing, and scrolling on short viewports. Decoration must not block interaction or reduce text readability.

The development asset gallery keeps its library/inspector layout and controls while sharing the game palette, Orbitron headings, square corners, and accessible controls. Debug text panels use the same palette and outlines.

The HTML UI uses React and Tailwind 4 and requires Safari 16.4+, Chrome 111+, or Firefox 128+, alongside the existing WebGL 2 requirement.

### 3D scene

The camera follows the Packet smoothly and adapts to the game area. Its orthographic view combines a 20-degree forward tilt with a fixed 5-degree lean from the right to reveal more wall faces. Maze rows and columns stay aligned with the screen, and the viewing angle stays constant as the Packet moves. The 1920×1080 view is the reference: other 16:9 sizes show the same amount of maze, while wider or narrower screens show more or less horizontally without stretching. Rendering interpolates movement between simulation updates, while portals and respawns show the destination immediately. The displayed camera aligns to rendered pixels to stabilize the maze outlines; slow panning can advance in tiny pixel steps. Gameplay renders at the display's native density up to a 3840×2160 pixel budget, then lowers its resolution through 1440p and 1080p budgets after sustained slow frames. Its final tiers render at five-sixths and two-thirds of the 1080p-or-native baseline, producing 900p and 720p on a 4K display while retaining lower fallbacks on smaller screens. These changes affect sharpness without changing item size or the visible maze area; menus and HUD retain browser-sharp text.

The maze walls and PACKETLOSS logo rise 12 units; the enemy prison's upper face sits at the same elevation. All three have dark shaded faces and crisp square outlines. Their outline colors distinguish them: muted pink for the maze, cyan for the prison, and gold for the logo. The lettering rises directly from the maze floor. The prison preserves its authored continuous rails with two rectangular openings per tile, extruded using the same geometry pipeline as the walls. The prison keeps its original top-face footprint while its vertical body and cyan side lines are compressed to a paper-thin sheet. The surrounding maze walls retain their faces and outer pink outlines; only pink edges touching the cyan prison bars are hidden. The prison retains its internal cyan bar outlines, without the outer cyan bounding box. Bars remain complete across enemy release paths; enemies retain their existing pass-through rules. All three use the same material finish and approximately 1.75-CSS-pixel outlines at the default zoom, with normal depth testing and antialiasing and no emission, brightness boosts, or bloom. Gameplay movement rules remain unchanged. All modes share the same pickup models and online palette: cyan data bits and gold power cores. Data bits are shallow cyan eight-tip stars with four long cardinal points, four shorter diagonal points, and darker bevels, matching the original point sprite. Power cores have their own solid, faceted gold 3D star, with long points on all three axes and shorter diagonal points, so they retain volume from every side. Their initial front tip points directly toward the game camera, and they turn horizontally around their own vertical axis once every six seconds, bringing the left tip forward to face the viewer each quarter-turn. They gently hover up and down twice per turn, with offset phases between powerups. Their gold facets catch the scene lighting as they rotate; collecting one absorbs the same 3D shape from its current hover and orientation. Rotation and hovering freeze while paused. The HUD, pause UI, and debug panels remain HTML overlays. A WebGL 2-capable browser is required.

The Packet is a shallow near-black beveled block hovering above the floor, with closely matched fixed colors on its top and sides, cyan corner outlines on the top and all four sides, depth edges, square pixel accents, and bright rectangular eyes mounted in shaded dark sockets on the upper surface visible from the game camera. Its presentation is enlarged by 15% while retaining its gameplay collision size. Four binary digits sit directly on the upper face among additional square pixel marks, staying attached and readable while independently switching between 0 and 1 with a soft flicker. Both eyes blink periodically and look toward actual movement, returning to center at rest. Collecting either kind of point triggers a 300 ms absorption: the cyan or gold star turns, contracts toward a tiny cyan point, and rises into the center of the Packet’s upper face over 180 ms. Its cyan corner outlines then pulse briefly while the body keeps its shape. Consecutive stars animate independently; pausing freezes absorption, and death or a teleport cancels pending intake from the old location. Its body is stretched 20% wider and stays aligned with the maze walls without rotational rocking, while ten surrounding floating binary digits form a tighter cluster around its center, face the camera, drift and fade independently, and update their 0/1 values at staggered intervals. Its simple silhouette echoes the maze outlines, with soft glow limited to the eyes. Three short speed marks trail behind actual movement in any direction and fade out within 120 ms of stopping; portals and respawns clear them immediately. Firewall is a brick cube with red joints, Virus a smaller dark sphere with thin orange spikes and pale tip highlights, Ping an orb with green segmented rings, Spam a stepped body with purple trim, and Lag a dark core wrapped in its original slim yellow rings. Firewall, Ping, and Spam have near-black body fills matching the Packet and pale highlights; Virus and Lag retain their original dark shell color. Quarantine is a purple polygonal containment sphere with rotating panels and orbiting pixels. Trojan is a red horse head with a long muzzle, pointed ears, arched neck, and segmented mane. Its disguise reuses the normal data-bit geometry: the horse contracts into the point on arrival and expands back out during reveal. Their separate GLBs share colored trim and gentle looping motion with periodic blinking. Eye shapes alone express their moods, without brow lines: Firewall, Virus, and Spam look angry, Ping has neutral upright eyes, and Lag has tired, half-closed eyes with a slight asymmetric droop. Edible enemies ease into wide, worried eyes and grey trim over 240 ms, and ease back when fear ends; the scared expression stays visible during both colors of the warning flashes. While enemies are edible, the Packet becomes a gold-outlined hunter with angular eyes and raised corner prongs, without a mouth. Its colors, eye shape, and raised details ease into and out of the hunter form over 320 ms. The gold outlines flicker as power is about to end. Eating an enemy draws a stream of bright pixels into the Packet while the enemy shell collapses; the shell’s shrink and spin ease in and out over this 420 ms animation, which finishes even when the last edible enemy is eaten. At the jail, the bug contracts while the original body eases back over 280 ms. The normal cyan form returns when no edible enemies remain and intake has finished. All character animation freezes when paused, while grounded shadows and existing collision sizes remain unchanged. The optimized enemy models load before gameplay starts; a missing enemy model produces a startup error.

## Maps

The Classic maze is `public/assets/mazes/default/maze.json`. Setting `VITE_GAME_ENV=DEMO` selects `public/assets/mazes/default/demo.json`; other values use the default authored maze. Endless generates its map independently of that selection. The [maze and tile atlas](MAZES.md) documents the authored layouts, generated sections, visual footprints, collision rules, and Quarantine wall joins.

Tiled map data defines walls, directional collision rules, portals, spawn locations, the enemy pen, and collectibles. The optional `enemyCount` selects up to seven original enemies in roster order; its default is seven. After editing `public/assets/mazes/default/demo.tmx` or `public/assets/mazes/tileset.tsx`, regenerate the demo JSON with `pnpm map:demo:convert`.

## Current scope

The playable Classic and Endless maze loops include scoring, lives, enemy recovery, portals, desktop/mobile controls, title and pause menus, terminal loss results, and device-local profiles and separate records. Classic also has compounding level progression and clear checkpoints. There is no audio, backend, authentication, cloud synchronization, or saved unfinished run. Development hotkeys still need a release review before public shipping.

Implementation details live in [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md); local checks and debugging shortcuts are in [Testing](TESTING.md).

Local development through `pnpm dev:full` also offers name-only **Play as guest** on the account and login screens. Each guest has an independent identity and persistent scores, restored through the browser refresh cookie; reusing a name never takes over another player. The local game server becomes ready automatically; guests do not need an owner startup or account cooldown. Local signup/reset passwords may be any non-empty string up to 128 characters. These shortcuts are absent from deployed account flows.

Local rooms automatically gain two randomly named BOT opponents, with a spare seat for a friend. They join, ready up, move toward pickups, and ready again after rematches; the human controls starting. Bots use the same authenticated network and gameplay rules as other players and leave after the last human's departure or reconnect reservation expires. The launcher accepts `--bots 0–3` (zero disables them). `--solo` instead disables bots and starts an unpersisted one-player Battle Royale immediately after **Create room**. Solo practice keeps movement, contractions, elimination, timeout, and reconnect behavior; production and ordinary local rooms still require two to four players. The `--multi` option skips account and room menus, restores a local identity or creates a Developer guest, and starts automatically after the configured bots are ready. Refresh replaces that creator’s previous room and starts a fresh match at stage zero. Pressing **C** closes the next ring immediately by advancing the closure countdown to its deadline; actors and effect timers do not fast-forward. The final ring cannot close further. These development matches do not save results. `--multi` requires one to three bots and excludes `--solo`. Bots, automatic development startup, and closure controls are unavailable in production.
